## 1. Executive Summary & Legal Licensing Framework

### 1.1 Licensing Analysis of Existing Instruments
A clinical audit of self-report questionnaires requires careful attention to intellectual property and licensing constraints:
- **ADHD-RS-IV (ADHD Rating Scale IV with Adult Prompts):** Authored by DuPaul, Power, Anastopoulos, and Reid / Barkley et al. Copyrighted by Guilford Press. Explicitly prohibits electronic reproduction, digital dissemination, scoring algorithm implementation, or web embedding without direct written permission and licensing royalty fees.
- **Barkley Adult ADHD Rating Scale-IV (BAARS-IV):** Authored by Russell A. Barkley, PhD. Copyrighted by Guilford Press. Strictly proprietary.
- **Conners Adult ADHD Rating Scales (CAARS) / Conners 4:** Copyrighted by Multi-Health Systems (MHS). Strictly proprietary and protected by trademark and copyright law.
- **WHO Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS v1.1):** Developed in conjunction with the World Health Organization. While the paper instrument is in the public domain for research and clinical practice, the World Health Organization and New York University hold trademark protections, and adapting the scale into modified computerized scoring must carry explicit attribution and must not alter the validated 6-item screening scoring weights.

### 1.2 ADHD Compass Independent Solution
To eliminate all copyright infringement, legal liability, and licensing restrictions while ensuring maximum clinical fidelity, **ADHD Compass authored original plain-language questions that directly represent the 18 established symptom domains defined in DSM-5-TR**:
- **9 Inattention Criteria:** DSM-5-TR Criterion A1(a) through A1(i).
- **9 Hyperactivity/Impulsivity Criteria:** DSM-5-TR Criterion A2(a) through A2(i).
- **Public Attribution Statement:** Transparently informs users and reviewing clinicians that these questions are original plain-language educational formulations based on the diagnostic criteria established in DSM-5-TR (APA 2022), and are not a substitute for a copyrighted clinical instrument.

## 3. Age-Specific Thresholds & Contextual Diagnostic Fact-Check

### 3.1 Age Threshold Discrepancy (5 vs 6 Symptoms)
- **APA DSM-5-TR (2022) Criterion A:**
  - Children and adolescents (up to age 16): Requires **at least 6 symptoms** in Inattention and/or **at least 6 symptoms** in Hyperactivity/Impulsivity persisting for at least 6 months.
  - Older adolescents and adults (aged 17 and older): Requires **at least 5 symptoms** in either category.
- **Fact-Check Action:**
  - Old test evaluated both adolescents and adults with identical 8-point thresholds based on an invented 88-point Likert sum.
  - Revised V2 implementation strictly asks the user their age (`Under 18` vs `18 or older`) and benchmarks reported symptom frequencies against the exact DSM-5-TR thresholds (≥ 5 for adults, ≥ 6 for youth).

### 3.2 Age of Onset Before Age 12 (DSM-5-TR Criterion B)
- **Clinical Standard:** "Several inattentive or hyperactive-impulsive symptoms were present prior to age 12 years." (DSM-5-TR Criterion B; Polanczyk et al. 2014).
- **Fact-Check Action:**
  - In the adult contextual branch, Question 25 explicitly inquires: *"Did you experience attention, restlessness, or disorganization difficulties before age 12?"*
  - The results page and clinician summary note this as an essential developmental prerequisite for differential diagnosis.

### 3.3 Cross-Setting Impairment (DSM-5-TR Criterion C)
- **Clinical Standard:** "Several inattentive or hyperactive-impulsive symptoms are present in two or more settings (e.g., at home, school, or work; with friends or relatives; in other activities)." (DSM-5-TR Criterion C).
- **Fact-Check Action:**
  - Question 24 in both youth and adult tracks explicitly verifies whether patterns occur across multiple environments or are confined strictly to a single stressful environment (e.g., only with a single teacher or boss).
  - Clinician discussion notes explicitly log cross-setting presence.

### 3.4 Functional Impairment (DSM-5-TR Criterion D)
- **Clinical Standard:** "There is clear evidence that the symptoms interfere with, or reduce the quality of, social, academic, or occupational functioning." (DSM-5-TR Criterion D).
- **Fact-Check Action:**
  - Question 26 evaluates the magnitude of everyday friction and interference across work, school, relationships, and self-care.

### 3.5 Differential Rule-Outs & Co-occurring Conditions (DSM-5-TR Criterion E)
- **Clinical Standard:** "The symptoms do not occur exclusively during the course of schizophrenia or another psychotic disorder and are not better explained by another mental disorder (e.g., mood disorder, anxiety disorder, dissociative disorder, personality disorder, substance intoxication or withdrawal)." (DSM-5-TR Criterion E; NICE NG87).
- **Medical Safety Fact-Check:**
  - Physical and psychological conditions that mimic ADHD inattention:
    1. Chronic sleep deprivation, obstructive sleep apnea, or circadian rhythm delay.
    2. Generalized anxiety disorder (racing thoughts, worry-induced distractibility).
    3. Major depressive disorder (pseudodementia, low cognitive stamina, apathy).
    4. Thyroid dysfunction (hypothyroidism causing brain fog; hyperthyroidism causing restlessness).
    5. Occupational burnout / chronic cognitive overload.
  - **Remediation:** Both `GettingDiagnosed.tsx` (Section 3 & Section 4) and the clinician notes in `ADHDTestPage.tsx` explicitly list these differential rule-outs and urge comprehensive clinical interviews.

## 4. Deletion of Invented Archetypes & Unscientific Claims

| Claim / Feature Reviewed | Source / Issue | Action Taken | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Total Score out of 88 (e.g. "64/88") | Unvalidated scoring sum without normative population standardization | **REMOVED** | Summing Likert scales into an arbitrary score creates a false sense of quantitative severity without clinical validity. |
| Alignment Percentage (e.g. "72% High Alignment") | Falsely mimics diagnostic probability | **REMOVED** | No self-report screener can yield a mathematical percentage probability of having ADHD. Violates clinical ethics. |
| Custom Archetype Archetypes (e.g. "Combined Phenotype", "Inattentive Phenotype") | Conflates formal DSM-5-TR diagnostic specifiers with self-administered quiz labels | **REMOVED** | Replaced with transparent reporting of symptom frequencies (e.g., "7 of 9 Inattentive patterns reported frequently"). |
| Individualized Neurochemical Claims ("Your prefrontal-striatal dopamine circuits...") | Mechanistic overreach | **REMOVED** | Individual self-assessments cannot measure or assert specific personal neurotransmitter levels or circuit dynamics. |
| Category headers during questionnaire ("Physical & Motor Restlessness") | Primes respondent bias and invalidates blinding | **REMOVED** | User requested removal of all category headers; only neutral question numbering and plain language are displayed. |

## 5. Non-Diagnostic Communication Phrasing Certification

### 5.1 Higher-Response Threshold
- **Wording Certified:**
  > *"You reported several patterns that can be considered during an ADHD evaluation. This does not mean that you have ADHD. A formal diagnosis requires an in-depth evaluation by a qualified healthcare professional who reviews your developmental history, examines symptom persistence across multiple life settings, and rules out other contributing factors."*
- **Clinical Rationale:** Validates the user's reported difficulties without generating unwarranted medical certainty or panic. Clearly defines the necessity of professional history-taking.

### 5.2 Lower-Response Threshold
- **Wording Certified:**
  > *"Your responses showed fewer of the patterns covered by this self-assessment. This does not rule out ADHD, nor does it mean your everyday difficulties are unimportant. ADHD can present subtly, masking may conceal symptoms, and other factors—such as chronic stress, sleep difficulties, or anxiety—can create similar challenges that still merit professional support."*
- **Clinical Rationale:** Directly adheres to user directive and APA clinical ethics. A negative self-screen must never be construed as ruling out ADHD or invalidating distress.
